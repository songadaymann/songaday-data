# Second opinions on the findings

Two skeptic passes re-ran every finding marked real or probably real, using different methods: same-week comparisons, year-month fixed effects, clustered standard errors, year-block bootstraps, and power checks for two of the superstition nulls. Their scripts are in `analysis/checks/` (`check_1_*`: confounds and data, `check_2_*`: statistics). Where they disagree with `findings.md`, trust this file.

## Pass 1: confounds and data

- `weekend_wild` holds up. Rerun in analysis/checks/check_1_weekend.py. The year x era difference reproduces at -10.35 points. Comparing weekend with weekdays inside the same ISO week (so slow tagging drift cancels) gives -10.34 points (SE 1.31): the weekend was lower in 561 weeks and higher in 338 (sign p<0.0001). Other controls: holding traveling fixed -10.1 (perm p=0.001); year-month x traveling -9.9; home songs only -10.2. Every one of the 18 years is negative, so are all 4 eras, and so are both the sheet part (-9.1) and the catalog-only part (-14.9). Songs uploaded the same UTC day give -11.0 (check_1_dates.py). One partial confound: weekday 'work' topics (Commission, Apple, crypto, politics, news songs) are 23.5% of weekday songs vs 16.0% of weekend songs and skew Excited/Silly. Dropping them shrinks the effect to -7.5 points, still p=0.001 and -7.1 within week. It is fewer Excited, Angry and Silly songs and more Tired, Sad, Chill and Pensive ones.
- `weekday_mood` holds up. check_1_weekend.py: with ISO-week strata (each day compared only with its own week) p=0.001; year x era x traveling p=0.001; dropping work/news topics p=0.003. Restricted to Monday-Friday the association disappears (p=0.61), so this is the weekend_wild finding said again, not a separate day-of-week pattern. **Corrected:** Yes, but it is only the weekend effect: weekends have fewer Excited, Angry and Silly songs and more Tired, Sad and Chill ones. Monday to Friday don't differ from each other (p=0.61).
- `travel_length` holds up. check_1_travel.py reproduces +17.3 s. Regression with month dummies plus genre and main-instrument dummies, clustered by trip (415 runs of travel days): +16.0 s, SE 2.8, z=5.8. Year-month strata +20.9 s. It shrinks but survives with genre strata (+11.5 s) and with event topics dropped (+10.3 s). By kind of trip: real trips over 100 km +22.6 s, family homes +11.4 s, between-homes and the 2020 family stay +8.5 s. Not uniform: the Brooklyn era shows -2.7 s; Berkeley, Jersey City and Hartford show +18 to +28 s.
- `travel_views` holds up. check_1_travel.py reproduces -27.7% within month. Other versions: year-month x YouTube channel strata -24.7%; event topics dropped -28.5%; between-homes and the 2020 family stay dropped -28.5% (shift p=0.001); month dummies plus age within the month -27.8% (trip-clustered z=-9.4). Every era is negative (-19% to -41%), and the within-month percentile rank is 0.064 lower. Real trips -32%, family stays -22%. Weaker in the full views model: within-month rank as the outcome gives p=0.07 (check_1_views_model.py), and on 2017-2026 alone p=0.26.
- `travel_mood` holds up. check_1_travel.py reproduces p=0.006 and gets stronger with each confound controlled. Adding season to the strata: p=0.001 (q about 0.005 in the 124-test family, check_1_requeue.py). Year-month strata p=0.0015. Dropping between-homes and the 2020 stay p=0.001; dropping family homes p=0.0005; real trips only vs home p=0.0005; event topics dropped p=0.0005. The overall association holds, but the 'biggest cell' changes with the subset (Anxious, Angry, Tired, Chill), so don't headline any single cell.
- `era_mood` **does not hold up**. check_1_era_trend.py. The published circular-shift null wraps 2026 back onto 2009, so its fake eras can't carry a slow trend, and that makes it anti-conservative here. Against 2,999 random contiguous four-segment partitions that don't wrap (each segment at least 365 songs), the real era partition's chi2 of 415.7 falls below the null median of 444.8: p=0.69, q about 0.86. A local test at each move (180 songs before vs after) gives a mean chi2 of 16.8 at the three real moves vs 20.7 at random dates (p=0.72). Percentiles among random dates: 37th, 7th and 76th. **Corrected:** No sign the homes matter. The mood mix drifts slowly over 17 years (Happy and Intense fade, Angry rises), and any split of the timeline into four contiguous stretches looks as different as the real home eras do. The moves themselves show no jump in mood.
- `beard_mood` holds up. check_1_beard.py reproduces V=0.084 (p=0.0005). It holds with year-month strata (p=0.0005), with year x era x traveling strata (p=0.0005), and with Clean vs Shadow only (V=0.145, p=0.0005). It holds separately in 2009-2014 and in 2015-2021 (each p=0.0005), with the same direction in both: Clean songs are less often Tired (3.2% vs 9.4%; 4.6% vs 7.4%) and more often Silly (20.1% vs 15.4%; 20.9% vs 12.1%). Caveats: the beard column is almost all 'Beard' from 2022 on, so this is a 2009-2021 result, and both tags are his own.
- `beard_views` holds up. check_1_beard.py. Within month, Clean vs not-Clean is +52% and Clean vs Shadow +51%. Adding traveling, weekday or channel to the strata gives +45% to +49%. Dropping event topics and news songs gives +51%; dropping the top 1% by views gives +42%. Clean songs beat the month's other songs in 111 of 149 months (sign p<0.0001). Against non-Clean songs within 3 days: mean +44%, median +11% (n=1,590). A views model clustered by month gives Clean x1.53 (p<0.0001), and x1.52 on 2009-2021 alone. Two cautions: there is no effect in the Berkeley era (-1.7%, 580 Clean songs), and event topics are twice as common on Clean days (15.3% vs 7.7%), which supports 'he shaves for occasions'. The occasions I can measure don't explain the effect, but unmeasured ones (shoots, gigs, production) can't be ruled out. It is an association, not a beard effect.
- `news_song_views` holds up. check_1_news.py reproduces +124%. One confound is real: songs with nouns or proper nouns tagged (42%) get +59% views within month, and 63.5% of news songs are tagged. Even so, the news effect is +83% inside tagged songs and +139% inside untagged ones, and x2.04 with tagging as a covariate (month-clustered z=7.1). Part of the effect is 'names famous things': non-news songs that match the news a year off get +67%. With match score and control score both in the model, the same-day score is x1.47 (z=6.8) and the control score x1.19 (z=2.0). Dropping the top 1%, Apple songs and Politics/Trump songs together still leaves +62%. Within-month rank 0.69 vs 0.51. Before descriptions existed +135%, after +89%.
- `news_politics_topic` **does not hold up**. check_1_news.py reproduces +1.80 points per 3 headlines (p=0.005). Dropping the 71 days around US elections and inaugurations: +1.42, p=0.0185, which is q=0.072 when swapped into the findings' own 124-p family (check_1_requeue.py). Also dropping 2009 to April 2010 (classifier-inferred sections): p=0.0175, q=0.068. Placebos using the headlines 7 or 14 days after the song, or 7 days before it, are not significant (p=0.18, 0.28, 0.55), so there is some same-day signal, but it doesn't reach the 'probably real' bar once elections are held out. **Corrected:** Mostly a US election-day effect. Within 3 days of a US federal election or 1 day of an inauguration, 32% of his songs are about politics, vs 9.5% on other days. Outside those windows the link to how much politics is in the news is weaker (+1.42 points per 3 headlines) and doesn't survive the multiple-test correction (q about 0.07). On the 15 days with the most politics headlines he wrote 3 politics songs.
- `trend_dark` **does not hold up**. check_1_era_trend.py reproduces +8.4 points per decade (Kendall p=0.0006). The rise is all Angry (2.2% to 9.9%, tau +0.73); Sad alone is flat (tau +0.05, p=0.76). Politics/Trump songs are 55.7% dark vs 12.9% for other songs, and grew from about 5% of songs to 12-31% after 2016. Dropping them: +3.6 points per decade, Kendall p=0.081 (q about 0.22). A linear probability model with politics as a covariate still gives +6.1 points per decade (year-clustered z=3.2), but that rests on only 18 clusters. Label drift: Intense went from 18.9% to 3.1% of songs; dark+Intense shows no trend (p=0.45) and Angry+Intense is flat (p=0.94). Five dark labels (Annoyed, Scary, Upset, In Pain, Sick) appear only from 2024. Restricted to the 2009-2021 sheet years: p=0.022, q about 0.08. **Corrected:** The share of dark-tagged songs jumped around 2016-17, from about 11% (2009-2015, no trend) to about 21% (2017-2026, no trend). That isn't a steady darkening, and the jump is Angry songs, mostly political. Sad songs are flat across all 17 years. Without Politics/Trump songs the trend is not significant (p=0.08), and the non-political dark share in 2025-2026 (8.6%, 9.7%) is back at or below 2009 (11.2%). Some of it may also be relabeling: Intense fell as Angry rose.
- `season_mood` **does not hold up**. check_1_season.py reproduces p=0.009-0.014 across 5 seeds. But the published null shuffles single months within a year, while moods drift in runs that last months, so any three neighbouring months look alike. A null that keeps seasons as 3 consecutive months and moves the season label at random each year gives p=0.080 (q about 0.22). Neither half is significant on its own (2009-2017 p=0.11, 2018-2026 p=0.39). Home songs only: p=0.042 (q about 0.15). What does replicate: the season x mood departures correlate r=+0.49 between the halves, and the winter Tired deficit appears in both (-2.5 and -1.9 points). It survives dropping December (p=0.004) and Christmas songs (p=0.009). **Corrected:** Suggestive at best: he writes slightly fewer Tired songs in winter (5.0% vs about 8%), in both halves of the data. The overall season-mood link isn't established once mood runs lasting more than a month are allowed for (p=0.08).
- `views_topic` holds up. check_1_views_model.py. The topic block is p<0.0001 with standard errors clustered by month and by year. It stays p<0.0001 with channel dummies and age within the month added, with the top 1% of songs dropped, and with within-month percentile rank as the outcome. It replicates in both 2009-2016 and 2017-2026 (each p<0.0001), and the topic multipliers correlate r=+0.88 between the halves (Apple x5.18 / x3.79; Conference x0.46 / x0.70).
- `views_mood` holds up. check_1_views_model.py. The mood block is p<0.0001 under month- and year-clustered errors, with channel and age-in-month added, with the top 1% dropped, and with rank as the outcome. It is p<0.0001 in each half, and the multipliers correlate r=+0.61 between halves (Silly x1.76 / x1.46; Excited x1.41 / x1.45; Angry x1.80 / x1.33). It shows even without the model: within-month views are Angry x1.77, Silly x1.52, Excited x1.51 vs Tired x0.62, Chill x0.73, Pensive x0.75.
- `views_length` holds up. check_1_length_views.py: with month dummies only, doubling the length gives x1.03 (month-clustered z=0.9), and the binned within-month shape is x0.97-x1.04 from 60 to 300 s, with songs under 60 s at x0.87. Adding topic, mood, genre or instrument alone gives x1.08-1.12; all together x1.20 (z=8.3). Silly, the second most-watched mood, has a median length of 102 s. In the full model it holds under month and year clustering, for songs over 60 s (x1.21), with the top 1% dropped and with rank as the outcome. It is much weaker in 2017-2026 (x1.07, block p=0.005). **Corrected:** Only once the kind of song is held fixed. Within a month, length on its own predicts almost nothing (x1.03 per doubling, not significant), and views are flat from 60 to 300 s. Short songs tend to be the Silly, topical ones, which get many views. Among songs of the same topic, mood, genre and instrument, a song twice as long gets about x1.20 the views, more in 2009-2016 (x1.33) than in 2017-2026 (x1.07).
- `views_genre` holds up. check_1_views_model.py. The genre block is p<0.0001 with month-clustered errors and p=0.0037 with year-clustered errors. It stays significant with channel and age-in-month added (p=0.0005), with the top 1% dropped (p=0.0001) and with rank as the outcome (p<0.0001). It is significant in both halves (p<0.0001 and p=0.014), with multipliers correlating r=+0.69 (Rock x1.42 / x1.19; Electro x1.21 / x1.30). It is real but small: 0.59% of within-month variance.

Data and method problems found:

- Spot-check (analysis/checks/check_1_spotcheck.py, seed 20261021): 20 random rows, 480 field comparisons against the public sheet CSV, the songaday-display catalog (newest file per chunk, deduped by n), the Open-Meteo cache (coordinates, mean temperature, precipitation, weather code), the YouTube snapshot (views) and moon illumination recomputed with PyEphem. 479 match. The one mismatch (#4968 length: dataset 159 s, sheet '239') is a typo for 2:39 in the sheet; the dataset agrees with the catalog, so the cleaning is right. No repeated song numbers, no gaps, no repeated video IDs. All 5,113 sheet date cells and 6,502 catalog dates agree with 2009-01-01 + (n-1). The 148 rows with duplicate titles are reused titles at least 12 songs apart, not duplicated rows.
- 44 songs were uploaded to YouTube before their song date (check_1_dates.py). #98-#107 were all uploaded 2009-04-03, 5-14 days early (mostly 'Ringtone for ...'). #733-#746, the Jan 2011 EagleShark/Hank series, went up 2011-01-02 to 01-07. #1675 went up 2013-06-06 for a 2013-08-02 song (57 days early) and #1028 5 days early. These are either pre-recorded batches or wrong video IDs. Their weather, weekday, sky and news joins describe the posting date rather than the recording day, and views.py's upload_delay <= 30 rule keeps them, because negative delays pass.
- The beard column is close to constant from 2022: Beard on 365/365 songs in 2022, 364/365 in 2023, 365/365 in 2025. 2009-2014 have almost no 'Beard' (2-36 songs a year), 2015-2016 are mostly Beard and 2017-2021 mostly Shadow. Only 151 of 213 months have more than one beard state, and only 3 of those are after 2021. So every beard result is really 2009-2021 Clean vs Shadow, which mostly means 'shaved that day', and Beard vs Shadow may just be a change in how he labels it.
- Mood vocabulary drifts. Intense went from 18.9% of songs in 2009 to 3.1% in 2026. Happy went from 40 songs in 2009 to 0-7 a year after 2012. Angry went from 2.2% to 9.9%. Annoyed, Scary, Upset, In Pain and Sick first appear in 2024-2026, in the catalog-only years. Year strata absorb this for most tests, but the trend and era tests can't.
- 'Traveling' (is_traveling) mixes trips with family stays. Of 1,328 traveling songs, 480 are at family homes (Avon, Canton, Southington CT; Severna Park MD), 87 are between homes or in the spring-2020 family stay, and 81 are under 30 km from home (Hackensack hospital, NYC in the Jersey City years). Only 667 are trips of more than 100 km.
- Nouns or proper nouns are tagged on 42% of songs (each about 24-25%), and coverage changes a lot by year: 71% in 2021, 5% in 2026. Tagged songs get +59% views within month, and the news-match flag depends on those fields, so news_match_* columns are not comparable across years with different tagging coverage.
- Two null models in analyze.py are anti-conservative. (1) The era tests (era_mood, and likewise era_minor, era_tempo and era_length) use a circular-shift null that wraps 2026 onto 2009, so a slow drift looks like an era effect. A non-wrapping random-partition null removes era_mood entirely (p=0.69). (2) The season and daylight block null shuffles single months, which ignores mood runs that last more than a month; a rotation null that keeps adjacent months together takes season_mood from p=0.012 to p=0.080.
- Minor: the public sheet CSV has 5,113 numbered rows (#5102-#5113 have blank beard and other cells), while dataset/README.md says the public sheet stops at #5101.
- The check scripts are in /Users/jonathanmann/SongADAO Dropbox/Jonathan Mann/projects/songaday-data/analysis/checks/: check_1_common.py (shared loader, which imports analyze.load_songs), check_1_spotcheck.py, check_1_weekend.py, check_1_travel.py, check_1_beard.py, check_1_era_trend.py, check_1_season.py, check_1_news.py, check_1_views_model.py, check_1_length_views.py, check_1_dates.py and check_1_requeue.py (recomputes BH q with a rerun p swapped into the 124-p family). The check_2_*.py files in that folder are not from this review.

## Pass 2: statistics

- `season_mood` **does not hold up**. The published null shuffles single months within each year, which breaks up slow drift inside a year. Moods drift over months, so any three adjacent months look more alike than three random ones, and that makes the real seasons look special. I reran analyze.py's chi-square with a null that rotates each year's months by a random 0-11 (seasons stay blocks of three adjacent months): V=0.064 vs chance 0.055, p=0.076. Using 2009-2025 only (2026 is a partial year): p=0.18. As a split-half check, I correlated the 48 season x mood cell residuals between odd and even years: r=+0.009, so the pattern does not replicate. With the rotation p, BH q=0.22. Even with the published p, Benjamini-Yekutieli gives q=0.27 and Holm 0.63. Scripts: analysis/checks/check_2_assoc.py, check_2_misc.py, check_2_fdr.py. **Corrected:** Noise. The season shows no stable effect on the mood mix: p=0.08-0.18 once seasons are kept as blocks of adjacent months, q of about 0.2, and odd and even years show unrelated patterns.
- `weekend_wild` holds up. I reran it as OLS with year, month-of-year and home-era fixed effects, SEs clustered by year-month: -10.36 pts [-12.96, -7.76], p=2e-13. With calendar-week fixed effects (each weekend compared only with its own week): -10.36 [-13.21, -7.52]. Clustered by year (18 clusters): p=6.5e-7. Year-block bootstrap, 2,000 draws: CI [-12.93, -7.84]. It holds at home only (-9.6) and in both halves of the timeline (2009-17 -7.6, p=0.0009; 2018-26 -13.2). It survives multiple-comparison correction: BH q=0.0011 on the 67 findings alone, BY q=0.009, Holm 0.013 (Holm can't go lower because 4,999 shuffles put a floor under p). Script: check_2_diffs.py.
- `weekday_mood` holds up. Cluster-robust stacked linear-probability Wald test (11 moods x 6 weekday dummies): with year x era fixed effects p(F)=5e-5, with week fixed effects p(F)=4e-5. Shuffling weekday labels only inside each calendar week gives V=0.058 vs chance 0.040, p=0.0002. The whole effect is weekend vs weekday, though. Monday-Friday only: p(F)=0.58. Saturday vs Sunday: p=0.11. Weekend vs weekday: p=1.5e-11. So this finding repeats weekend_wild and is not separate evidence. Scripts: check_2_assoc.py, check_2_misc.py. **Corrected:** Real, but it is only the weekend effect: weekend moods differ from weekday moods, and the five weekdays don't differ from each other (p=0.58).
- `travel_mood` holds up. Cluster-robust stacked Wald test of travel on 11 mood indicators. With year x era fixed effects, clustered by month: p(F)=0.0037. With year-month fixed effects: p(F)=1.8e-6. analyze.py's circular shift with year-month strata instead of year x era: V=0.059 vs chance 0.035, p=0.0006. Clustered by year (18 clusters): chi-square p=0.00024, but the small-sample F version is p=0.37, because 11 restrictions on 18 clusters leaves almost no power. BH q stays below 0.05 (0.026 on the 67 findings). BY over the 124 published tests gives q=0.15, so call it probably real, not real. **Corrected:** Probably real (q of about 0.03 under BH). It does not survive the stricter BY correction (q=0.15).
- `travel_length` holds up. OLS with year, month-of-year and era fixed effects, clustered by month: +17.3 s [+11.4, +23.2]. With year-month fixed effects: +20.9 s [+14.8, +27.1]. Clustered by year: p=0.0002. Year-block bootstrap CI: [+10.3, +24.2]. On a log scale: +15.4% [+10.6, +20.4]. With unclipped lengths: +21.6 s. It survives BY (q=0.009). Script: check_2_diffs.py.
- `travel_views` holds up. log10 views regressed on travel with year-month fixed effects, clustered by month: -27.7% [-33.7, -21.1]. Adding weekday fixed effects: -26.6%. Clustered by year: p=4.5e-5. Year bootstrap CI: [-36.0, -20.0]. In the full views model, the travel block's within-month permutation p is 0.001 (the floor), month-clustered p=0.00035 and year-clustered p=0.004. Scripts: check_2_diffs.py, check_2_views.py.
- `era_mood` **does not hold up**. The null used is anti-conservative. The circular shift (at least a year) wraps one era from 2026 round to 2009, joining the two ends of a series whose mood tags drift. That shrinks chance chi-square values, so the real, unbroken eras look special. Reproduced, the shift null gives p=0.0012. Under a null that cuts the timeline at 3 random points into 4 unbroken eras of at least 365 songs each: V=0.146 vs chance 0.149, p=0.69. Sliding the real era boundaries together by common offsets (372 offsets): p=0.58. Splitting a single home into 4 equal unbroken chunks gives V=0.155 for Hartford and 0.157 for Jersey City, more than the 0.146 between homes. With the corrected p, q=0.86. Script: check_2_assoc.py. **Corrected:** Noise. Mood tags drift steadily over 17 years, but the moves between homes mark no break beyond that drift: any four stretches of time differ as much (p=0.69).
- `beard_mood` holds up. Cluster-robust stacked Wald test (2 beard dummies x 11 moods), clustered by month. With year x era fixed effects: p(F)=1e-9. Within the same month (year-month fixed effects): p(F)=7.7e-13. Within the same week: p(F)=1.1e-10. analyze.py's circular shift with year-month strata: V=0.082 vs chance 0.029, p=0.0002. Statistically solid. Beard and mood are both his own tags, usually entered together, and these tests can't rule out that tagging habit. Beard tags change about 1,520 times (runs average about 4 days), so the 'beards last months' rationale for the shift doesn't fit the data, but the result holds either way.
- `beard_views` holds up. log views regressed on Shadow and Clean dummies (Beard as baseline) with year-month fixed effects: Shadow +13%, Clean +69%. Joint p=1e-12 clustered by month, p=0.0011 clustered by year. In the full views model the beard block has partial R^2 0.75% vs a within-month permutation null of 0.05% (p=0.001, the floor), month-clustered p=5.7e-7, year-clustered p=0.0024, year-bootstrap Wald p=2e-5. The effect is real. The published magnitudes (Beard -3%, Clean +24%) are anova adjusted means against each month's average, diluted by months with no beard variation. Compared directly within the month, Clean is about +69% over Beard (+44% with the other features held fixed). Script: check_2_views.py. **Corrected:** Real. Clean-shaven songs get about 1.7x the views of bearded songs from the same month (1.44x with topic, mood and the rest held fixed). The published '+24%' understates the gap.
- `news_politics_topic` **does not hold up**. The effect size reproduces: +1.80 pts per 3 politics headlines. But the within-month permutation treats days as interchangeable, while politics headlines and politics songs both bunch on consecutive days. With year-month fixed effects and SEs clustered by month: p=0.012 [+0.40, +3.19]. With year and month fixed effects, clustered by year: p=0.016. With the clustered p in place of the published one, BH q=0.049-0.050 (published 0.023). Year clustering gives q of about 0.06. Dropping +-3 days around the 8 US general elections (56 songs): +1.48, p=0.037. Dropping every November: p=0.029. BY over the published p: q=0.12. The year bootstrap CI ([+0.63, +3.11]) excludes 0, but a percentile bootstrap over 18 years tends to understate uncertainty. The finding sits on the q=0.05 line and depends heavily on election days. **Corrected:** Suggestive, not established: about +1.8 points of politics songs per 3 extra politics headlines (95% CI +0.4 to +3.2, clustered p of about 0.012-0.016, q of about 0.05). Without election weeks it weakens to p=0.04.
- `news_song_views` holds up. Regression with year-month fixed effects, clustered by month: +124% [+85, +171]. Adding weekday fixed effects: +120%. Clustered by year: p=7e-7. Year bootstrap CI: [+86, +175]. In the views model: permutation p=0.001, month-clustered p=1e-4, year-clustered p=0.0011. Because news-song detection needs text fields, I also restricted the comparison to songs that have proper nouns or a description: +76% [+39, +122], p=4e-6. Before #4749 (no descriptions): +135%. Real, though about +75% is the fairer like-for-like size. **Corrected:** Real. News songs get roughly 1.8-2.2x the views of same-month songs; about +76% when compared only with songs that have text the news matcher could read.
- `trend_dark` holds up. Theil-Sen: +8.2 pts/decade [+3.4, +13.7]. Residual lag-1 autocorrelation is only +0.12, so the AR(1)-adjusted p is 0.003. Newey-West p=0.0005. Song-level OLS clustered by year: +8.6 pts/decade, p=0.0002. Without the partial 2026: +9.5, p=0.0005. A random-walk test (mean of the year-on-year changes) gives p=0.76, but it depends only on the first and last years and has almost no power, and the low residual autocorrelation favours a steady trend. Survives BY (0.02) and Holm (0.036). It is entirely Angry + Anxious: those two rise +6.8 pts/decade (p=0.0004). Sad alone is flat (+0.4, p=0.72), and 'dark' without Angry and Anxious is +1.7 (p=0.18). Script: check_2_trend.py. **Corrected:** Real as a trend in the tags, but it is more Angry and Anxious songs, not sadder ones: Sad has stayed at about 4-13% a year with no trend.
- `views_topic` holds up. Rebuilt the published design. Partial R^2 is 8.10%, against a null of 0.42% mean (99th percentile 0.69%) when topic rows are shuffled within months: permutation p=0.001 (floor). Month-clustered Wald p=3e-28. Year-bootstrap Wald p of about 0 (there are too few years to cluster on with 30 terms). Apple x4.70, year-bootstrap CI x2.78-x7.15. Without Apple the block still gives p=3e-24. Script: check_2_views.py.

Data and method problems found:

- analyze.py's circular-shift null with no strata (era_mood, era_minor, era_tempo, era_length) wraps one era from 2026 back to 2009. For any outcome that drifts over time this shrinks the chance statistic and makes the p-values too small. Use random unbroken cut points instead: era_mood goes from p=0.0014 to p=0.69.
- The month-block null for the seasonal tests (season_mood, season_tempo, daylight_sad, daylight_minor) shuffles single months within a year and destroys month-to-month adjacency. Moods drift within a year, so this makes the p-values too small. Rotating each year's months instead raises season_mood's p from 0.012 to 0.076.
- Within-month permutation tests treat days as interchangeable. Headlines and topical songs bunch on consecutive days, so the news tests (e.g. news_politics_topic) look stronger than they are: p=0.0048 by permutation vs 0.012-0.016 with clustered SEs.
- beard_views reports anova adjusted means (Beard -3%, Shadow -13%, Clean +24%). These are diluted by months with no beard variation. A within-month fixed-effects regression gives Clean about +69% over Beard (+44% in the full views model).
- The FDR family pads 67 findings with 57 near-duplicate raw and model diagnostics. That barely moves BH here (17 discoveries either way), but the tests are dependent. Under Benjamini-Yekutieli, season_mood (q=0.27), travel_mood (0.15) and news_politics_topic (0.12) fail. With 4,999 shuffles, every floor p (0.0002) maps to the same q of 0.00165, so the 'real' findings can't be ranked by strength.
- weekday_mood is not separate from weekend_wild: Monday-Friday moods don't differ (p=0.58), so the two findings count the same effect twice.
- trend_dark ('moods getting darker') is entirely more Angry and Anxious tags; Sad is flat (p=0.72). Part of it may be a shift in his tagging vocabulary, which the report should say in the headline.
- news_song detection needs text (descriptions only from #4749, proper nouns on about a quarter of songs), so news songs are compared partly with songs the matcher couldn't read. Restricted to songs with text, the effect is +76%, not +124%.
- The beard_mood circular-shift rationale says beards last months, but the beard3 tag changes about 1,520 times in 6,456 songs (runs of about 4 days). The result survives within-month and within-week tests anyway.
- Of the 13 diff and slope fun nulls with a published null SD, several had too little power to show anything. supermoon_wild (69 songs) needs about 17 points for 80% power, friday13_dark (32 songs) about 18 points, eclipse_dark about 12 points, mercury_views about 20% views. These are 'can't tell', not 'shows nothing'.
