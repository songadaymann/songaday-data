# The Song A Day dataset

Jonathan Mann has written and recorded a song every day since January 1, 2009. This folder has
every song as data, plus what was happening around it: where it was made and the weather
there, the moon and planets, the news, and how many people have watched it.

**`songaday.csv` is the one to start with.** It has one row per song and 121 columns, and it
joins all the other files here. As of 2026-10-21 it has 6,502 songs: #1 (2009-01-01) to #6502
(2026-10-20), with no gaps. Song #N is always dated 2009-01-01 + (N - 1) days.

Everything here is generated by the scripts in [`../sources/`](../sources/README.md). Don't
edit these files by hand; fix the script and rerun it.

`songaday.csv` leaves out four long text columns that stay in their own files: songs.csv's
`generated_summary` (a machine-written summary of the lyrics for #1-#4748, not Jonathan's
words), news.csv's `top_items` (that day's top five headlines) and news_matches.csv's
`matched_items` (the headlines a song matched). It also leaves out places.csv's `songs`,
`first_date` and `last_date`. Join on `number` or `date` to get them back.

## Reading it

```python
import pandas as pd

songs = pd.read_csv("dataset/songaday.csv", parse_dates=["date"])
songs["topics"].str.split("|")              # list columns are "|"-separated
songs.groupby("home_era")["is_home"].mean()
songs[songs["sky_is_full_moon_day"]].groupby("mood").size()
```

Conventions:

- **Blank means unknown or none.** There are no `NaN`, `null` or `n/a` strings. Read with
  `keep_default_na=False` to keep blanks as empty strings.
- **Booleans** are `True` or `False`. A few (`is_major`, `is_minor`) are blank when unknown,
  so pandas reads them as objects instead of bools.
- **Lists** (`topics`, `instruments`, `styles`, `nouns`, `proper_nouns`, `song_flags`,
  `news_matched_terms`) are separated by `|`, main item first. `sky_retrograde_planets` and
  `sky_conjunctions` use `;`.
- **Dates** are ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`. Song dates are Jonathan's local date.
- **Text** is UTF-8. `description` can contain line breaks inside quotes, so count rows with
  a CSV reader, not `wc -l`.
- **Prefixes** say where a column came from: none for the song itself, `place_` (places.csv
  and weather.csv), `weather_`, `sky_`, `yt_` (YouTube), `fan_` (songaday.fan) and `news_`.
  Within each source the original column name is kept.

## Known caveats

**Correlations.** There are 121 columns, so hundreds of pairs will look related by chance.
Test the hypothesis you started with, correct for the number of comparisons, and check the
control (a year earlier or later, or a shuffled date). Most columns also move with time: the
home era drives the place, weather and season mix; views fall with the song's year; the sheet
recorded things differently in different years. Compare within a year or an era before
believing a pattern.

**The song data.**
- The public spreadsheet stops at #5101 (2022-12-19). Songs #5102 on come only from the
  catalog of Jonathan's working spreadsheet, which has no separate main-instrument or
  main-style column; the first list item is used, which matched every song before #5102.
- The cleaning made judgment calls (Improvised merged into Improv, "Bass Drums" split into
  Bass and Drums, and so on). They're in the alias tables in `sources/songs.py`. Cells worth
  fixing in the sheet itself are listed in `../analysis/sheet-fixes.csv` (615 cells).
- `nouns` and `proper_nouns` are filled on only about a quarter of songs, and `description`
  only from #4749 on. A blank means "not tagged", not "none".
- `mode` is unknown for 443 songs. 429 of them fall in the runs #212-#271 and #358-#727, where
  the sheet wrote only the root, so a minor key looks major. Don't count those as major.
- Only the 40 YouTube IDs the sources disagreed on were checked live. Some video titles carry
  an off-by-one song number.

**Places and home.**
- `place` is a hand mapping with some guesses, all explained in `place_note`: Vienna is
  Vienna, Austria; "Utah" (#1044) is taken to be Patagonia, AZ, and "New Zealand" (#748)
  Berkeley; states with no town use the capital as a stand-in. 110 songs have
  `place_confidence` low. Filter on it when the exact place matters.
- `place_kind` describes the Location string, not the song: "home" means the string names
  his home town, and his studios are "studio". Use `is_home` for "was he home".
- The home eras and the "between homes" stretches are dated by hand from the songs; the
  evidence is in `ERAS` in `sources/merge.py`, and the script checks that each boundary falls
  on a song at that home. "Home" means the home town, not the apartment. In the Brooklyn
  years all of New York City counts, because the sheet's "NYC" sometimes means Brooklyn.
  His family in Avon and Canton, CT (13-19 km from Hartford) and Severna Park, MD counts
  as traveling. "In The Car" songs count by their borrowed place; most are local drives.
- From May to mid-July 2020 he was mostly with family (Severna Park, then Avon). Those songs
  are in the jersey_city era and count as traveling, though they're closer to a move than a
  trip.

**Weather.**
- Values are for the reanalysis grid cell around the place (about 9-25 km), on the place's
  local day, not for the room the song was recorded in.
- "drizzle" (1,451 songs) often means only a trace of rain; use
  `weather_precipitation_sum` for thresholds. ERA5 never produces thunderstorm codes, so
  "storm" never appears.
- The last week of songs (#6496-#6502) use preliminary data that `weather.py` replaces on
  its next fetch more than a week later.

**Sky.**
- Every sky value is for 17:00 New York time, even when he was in Berkeley, Vienna or
  Phuket. Phase, station and eclipse days are New York calendar days, so 9 of the 81
  eclipses land a day before their UTC date.
- An eclipse day means visible somewhere on Earth, not where he was. Faint penumbral lunar
  eclipses count, as in NASA's catalogs.
- Conjunctions and the alignment arc use ecliptic longitude only. The supermoon definition
  is one of several, and others give a different count.

**YouTube.**
- Views are a snapshot (2026-10-18), not live, and 4 counts are older (on or before
  2026-08-04, for videos that are no longer public, including #4145 with 923,570). #6501 and
  #6502 came out after the snapshot.
- Views are extremely uneven: the top 1% of songs (65) have 75% of all 39.3 million views,
  #3974 "Baby Yoda Baby Baby Yoda" alone 25%, and the median song has under 400. Use logs or ranks.
- Old songs have had more time; divide by `yt_days_online`. 13 videos were uploaded more than
  30 days after their song (#1-#4 in 2021), so their counts cover a shorter life.
- Songs #508 and #536 were linked to the same video. songs.csv gives it to #536, so #508 has
  no video and blank counts; #536's `yt_note` still says "same video as song 508".
- YouTube's API Services policies limit how long data fetched from the API may be kept
  without refreshing. Refresh `views.csv` (`sources/views.py --refresh-youtube` with a key)
  before relying on the counts for long.

**songaday.fan ratings.** Very thin: 5,445 of 6,502 songs have no votes and only 16 have
three or more, mostly the first fifteen songs. Treat `fan_score` as noise for almost every song.

**News.**
- The Portal is written by volunteers, and how much they wrote down changes by year (12
  items a day in 2009, 22 in 2010, 10 in 2014). `news_n_items` measures coverage, not how much
  happened.
- 2009 to about April 2010 has no section headings, so those section counts are a
  classifier's guesses (81.5% accurate on held-out items); see `news_sections_inferred`.
- The Portal files events under roughly their UTC date and songs carry Jonathan's local
  date, which is why the match window is the song's day and the two days before.
- Matching is plain string matching. It misses songs that refer to an event without naming
  it ("the election") and catches some coincidences (a surname shared with someone in the
  news). Compare `news_match_score` with `news_control_score`, or rank by
  `news_match_excess`.
